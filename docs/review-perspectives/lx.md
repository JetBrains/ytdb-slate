# Licensing reviewer

**Definition.** Licensing design quality means traceable permission and conditions from each in-scope external input to its published output without treating records as a legal guarantee.

**Charter.** Identify copied, adapted, generated, or third-party material. Check its provenance, permission basis, and compliance with every applicable condition. Use prefix `LX`.

**Design-quality questions**

1. Does the publication or adaptation path keep each in-scope item's permission basis traceable to the use that basis permits?
2. Does the publication structure keep required conditions associated with the material they govern in the shipped result, for example attribution, notices, license text, source links, or modification notices?
3. Does the handling of unknown, conflicting, or limited rights make those limits visible to the user or qualified decision maker rather than turn uncertainty into assumed permission?
4. Can the existing provenance and condition evidence stay traceable through the source, package contents, and publication path without unnecessary maintenance work?

**Examples of useful evidence**

These are examples, not required checks or artifacts. Use evidence relevant to the approved requirement and charter.

- Source and version records, retrieved licenses, copied-portion records, and dependency inventories.
- Written permission, package manifests, shipped notices, and recorded unresolved permission questions.
